# Story development

Use the current brief. If ideas are needed, offer ten numbered ideas unless the user requests another count. Each gets a curiosity-driven title, a spoken hook, the ending twist, two or three animatable moments, and a clear fiction/history/disputed/folklore label. Mix subjects and eras when the brief is open. Favour a surprising physical cause and effect, an understandable character objective, escalating stakes and a visual payoff; keep the world buildable from simple geometry and character cards.

Verify key facts before presenting a historical idea as true, using authoritative sources. A source's popularity does not establish accuracy. Do not present the supplied soldier example as documented history. Fiction should be plainly labeled and need not masquerade as a researched event.

After the user selects an idea, write narration without stage directions. A rough starting estimate is 3.7 words/second: about 75 words for 20 seconds, 110 for 30 seconds, 220 for 60 seconds. This is a drafting estimate, not an audio timing guarantee. Hook immediately, establish who/where, build two to four causal beats, pivot to the underlying mechanism, then finish on the payoff. Use short spoken sentences and contractions; avoid narrating every visible detail.

Deliver a copyable script, word count, estimated length and one paragraph of visual intent. Recommend a steady, warm reading with natural sentence-end pauses and no background music. WAV or MP3 is supported. Ask for the final voiceover and its actual text if they are not supplied. Do not require a paid TTS service or automatically rewrite a supplied final script.
